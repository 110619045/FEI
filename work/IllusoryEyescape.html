Illusory Eyescape externalizes the viewer's attention through eye-tracking and generative systems, turning perception into evolving images that question how meaning emerges between human and machine.

This interactive art installation transforms eye-tracking data into generative images. The middle image was generated from the words "Mouse," "Apple," "Computer," "Drink," "Technical," "Art," "Animal," and "Time," based on a total reading duration of 30 seconds. Each red dot represents a gaze point recorded at 200-millisecond intervals, visualizing how the viewer's attention shaped the final image.

Motivation

As generative systems increasingly produce images that resemble artistic creation, the boundary between human and machine authorship becomes blurred.
If a computer can generate images that look like art, what distinguishes them from human-made works?


This led me to question what defines an artwork.
Is it the visual outcome, or something beyond the image itself?


I began to realize that what we often attribute to art is not just the image, but the presence of consciousness.
This consciousness operates on two levels: the author's consciousness embedded in the act of creation, and the viewer's consciousness that interprets and gives meaning to the work.
Together, these layers shape how art is experienced and understood.

Yet these processes remain invisible.
While consciousness plays a central role in how we experience and define art, it cannot be directly seen.

Concept

Rather than defining what art is, this project shifts the focus to how meaning is formed.

What if consciousness—
both in creation and perception—
could be externalized and made visible?

Illusory Eyescape explores this possibility by collapsing the boundary between author and viewer.
The participant becomes both the creator and interpreter of the work.

At its core, the work is grounded in the idea that meaning does not reside in systems or language, but emerges through experience.

Consciousness

Human consciousness is fundamentally different from machine processing. It is shaped by subjective experience and cannot be fully replicated or externally accessed. Each individual's perception forms a unique and irreducible perspective.

Language

Language serves as a primary medium for expressing inner experience, yet it cannot fully capture it. Meaning is not fixed within language itself, but emerges through its use and interpretation.

Vision

Vision is a central channel through which consciousness is formed and expressed. The act of looking is both a way of perceiving and projecting awareness. Gaze becomes a traceable manifestation of otherwise invisible mental processes.

From Concept to System


Here, gaze becomes the point where consciousness is made visible.
By transforming gaze into a generative input, the system turns the act of looking into a creative force

To make consciousness observable, the system translates perception into a sequence of transformations.

GAZE   is used as a proxy for attention, capturing how participants engage with content in real time.
Because subjective experience cannot be directly accessed, gaze becomes a trace of individual perception—both receiving and projecting awareness.

Text   serves as an intermediate layer, where perception can be structured and filtered, and transformed. expression.
As participants read, their attention shapes the selection and transformation of words, with language acting as a bridge between inner experience and external expression.

Image   becomes the final output, translating these perceptual traces into a visible form.

Through these decisions, the system transforms abstract ideas into a tangible interaction, where perception, language, and vision converge.

System

The system operates in four key stages:

  • Stage 1 - Perception Capture

    • Gaze is tracked in real time as the participant reads, capturing the invisible traces of attention as raw data.

  • Stage 2 - Attention Filtering

    • The system extracts key linguistic fragments based on the duration and frequency of fixations.

  • Stage 3 - Generative Transformation

    • Selected text is translated into visual form, externalizing perceptual traces through a generative process.

  • Stage 4 - Reflective Feedback

    • The generated image re-enters the experience, reshaping the participant's attention and initiating a new cycle of perception.

Design Decisions

Reading Material

Designing words as a system of contextual meaning

Due to the constraints of the text-to-image model, English was chosen as the primary language.
Words were selected from educational vocabulary to ensure familiarity, but more importantly, they were manually curated and structured to support semantic relationships.

Rather than functioning as isolated units, each word exists within a network of associations.
Its meaning shifts depending on context—emerging through its relation to surrounding words.

For example:
"Apple" with "Fruit" → a fruit
 "Apple" with "Computer" → a brand

This design reflects the idea that language does not carry fixed meaning, but is continuously reinterpreted through context.

Reading Interface

The reading interface is designed to guide how participants engage with the text.

In the initial layout, all words were presented evenly.
User testing revealed that participants struggled to identify where to begin, often leading to confusion during the reading process.

To address this, the interface was redesigned with a clear visual hierarchy.
Larger words act as entry points, while smaller words form supporting connections.

This shift improves both readability and visual attraction, while also reframing language as something to be interpreted rather than passively consumed.
The layout draws from the structure of a newspaper, subtly suggesting that meaning is not fixed, but constructed through reading.

From passive reading
to active interpretation

Data Extraction

The system focuses on fixation — the moment when attention is held.

Following research on ocular behavior, the system prioritizes Fixations (200-500ms) over Saccades, as they represent the window where the brain processes information.

Instead of treating all gaze equally, it focuses on how long a viewer looks at a word.
This approach is informed by eye-tracking research, where information is primarily processed during fixations rather than saccades.

During reading, words respond dynamically to gaze.
When a word is looked at:
– It becomes larger
 – After 1 second, it is highlighted
 – After 1.5 seconds, it is recorded

At the same time, related words are lightly marked.
This creates connections between words, allowing meaning to build through attention.

Through this process, reading becomes an active experience.

This figure shows how gaze influences text interaction within the "language game." When a viewer fixates on a word (e.g., "Time"), the word is first enlarged to indicate attention. After one second, it is highlighted with a black background, confirming focused reading. At the same time, semantically related words (e.g., "Spring" and "Watch") are activated and displayed with a grey background. This mechanism visualizes how attention not only selects a word, but also triggers connections within a network of meanings.

Visual Framing

Instead of presenting generated images as independent outputs, the system embeds them within the pupil of the eye.

This decision reinforces the duality of seeing and being seen.
The viewer looks at the image, while the image appears to look back.

The use of an eye form frames perception as something both internal and externalized, positioning the generated image as a projection of consciousness.

The visual structure resonates with the duality explored in Magritte's The False Mirror, where seeing and being seen collapse into a single frame.

Implementation

The system is implemented as a real-time pipeline that captures, processes, and transforms gaze into generative output.

Eye Tracking

Gaze is tracked using WebGazer.js

Real-time gaze data is captured through a standard webcam, revealing where attention lingers.

Processing

Fixation filtering & word extraction

Words are selected based on how long they are viewed, focusing on moments of cognitive processing.

Generation

DALL·E to generate images

Selected words are used as prompts to generate images, forming a feedback loop with perception.

// Reflections

"This journey marked a pivotal shift in my research philosophy. In the past, I focused on optimizing existing systems.
Now, I understand that creating new experiences is, in itself, a way of understanding the world."

While the discourse on humans and machines is often dominated by statistics and optimization, as someone who thrives on challenging the status quo, I didn't want to just categorize how people feel about AI; I wanted to create an open-ended dialogue. This led to the creation of Illusory Eyescape.

Consciousness is notoriously difficult to define, let alone visualize. My core realization through this project was that the most powerful way to discuss the invisible is through the power of lived experience. By using the viewer's own gaze as a medium, I transformed a philosophical inquiry into a tangible, participatory encounter.

The greatest challenge of this project lay in navigating vast, unfamiliar territories to build a rigorous conceptual foundation. It wasn't enough to simply understand these theories; I had to decide: Which visual elements would best embody "consciousness"? What specific technologies could capture the ephemeral nature of a gaze? This required a constant iterative loop between high-level conceptualization and low-level technical execution.

Balancing the rigor of data with the fluidity of art was daunting, but this transparent connection became more than a technical bridge between human and machine; it is my blueprint for a future where technology doesn't just function, but deeply resonates with the human spirit.
