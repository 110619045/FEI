Illusory Eyescape externalizes the viewer's attention through eye-tracking and generative systems, transforming subjective perception into evolving images to question how consciousness and meaning shift within human–machine interaction.

This interactive art installation transforms eye-tracking data into generative images. The middle image was generated from the words “Mouse,” “Apple,” “Computer,” “Drink,” “Technical,” “Art,” “Animal,” and “Time,” based on a total reading duration of 30 seconds. Each red dot represents a gaze point recorded at 200-millisecond intervals, visualizing how the viewer’s attention shaped the final image.

Theoretical Grounding and Creative Rationale

Consciousness

Subjective experience is shaped by perception and memory and cannot be fully replicated or externally observed. Thomas Nagel’s question "What is it like to be a bat?" highlights the limits of external understanding. Consciousness remains fundamentally personal and nonreplicable. Our work begins with the idea of individual consciousness, focusing on the uniqueness of perception rather than whether machines can truly be conscious.

Visual Experience

Vision also plays a central role in shaping perception. Magritte’s The False Mirror serves as a visual metaphor for the ambiguity of seeing and being seen, reinforcing the idea that perception is always filtered through subjectivity. In our work, the gaze becomes both input and reflection. The irreplaceable nature of visual experience forms a key part of conscious awareness and is central to the visual language of this work.

The Language

Wittgenstein’s early work suggests that language defines the limits of what can be thought and expressed. In contrast, his later work, emphasizes that meaning is shaped through context and everyday use. These views reveal both the potential and constraints of language in conveying inner experience. Language limits thought but also enables it. It is not only an output of consciousness but also a force that shapes perception.

System Workflow

The system operates in four key stages:

  • Stage 1 - Consciousness Extraction and Data Storage

    • Eye-tracking captures fixations during reading. Text dynamically responds to gaze, and key words are stored.

  • Stage 2 - Text Input for Image Generation

    • Selected words are sent to the DALL·E for image generation.

  • Stage 3 - Generative Transformation

    • DALL·E returns a visual representation of the viewer’s fixated words as a reflection of the viewer’s consciousness.

  • Stage 4 - Output and Interactive Update

    • The generated image replaces the previous content and becomes a new artwork that continuously evolves through further interactions with viewers.

// Reflections

"This journey marked a pivotal shift in my research philosophy. In the past, I focused on optimizing existing systems.
Now, I understand that creating new experiences is, in itself, a way of understanding the world."

While the discourse on humans and machines is often dominated by statistics and optimization, as someone who thrives on challenging the status quo, I didn't want to just categorize how people feel about AI; I wanted to create an open-ended dialogue. This led to the creation of Illusory Eyescape.

Consciousness is notoriously difficult to define, let alone visualize. My core realization through this project was that the most powerful way to discuss the invisible is through the power of lived experience. By using the viewer’s own gaze as a medium, I transformed a philosophical inquiry into a tangible, participatory encounter.

The greatest challenge of this project lay in navigating vast, unfamiliar territories to build a rigorous conceptual foundation. It wasn't enough to simply understand these theories; I had to decide: Which visual elements would best embody "consciousness"? What specific technologies could capture the ephemeral nature of a gaze? This required a constant iterative loop between high-level conceptualization and low-level technical execution.

Balancing the rigor of data with the fluidity of art was daunting, but this transparent connection became more than a technical bridge between human and machine; it is my blueprint for a future where technology doesn't just function, but deeply resonates with the human spirit.
